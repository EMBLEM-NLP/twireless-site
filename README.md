## Change something

1. Edit `src/site.json`, or a page in `src/pages/` (design 1), `designs/design-2/pages/` (design 2) or `designs/design-3/pages/` (design 3).
2. Run `python3 build.py` (design 1, into `dist/`), `python3 build.py --design design-2` (into `dist-design-2/`) or `python3 build.py --design design-3` (into `dist-design-3/`).
3. Upload the new folder.

Hours, phone number, email and address are typed once in `site.json` and appear on every page of every design, in the open/closed signs (the neon sign in design 1, the "Open until 8 p.m." tag on design 2's hero photo) and in the search-engine data.

An offer in `site.json` looks like this:

```json
{
  "brand": "Bell",
  "category": "mobile",
  "title": "New phone on a Bell plan",
  "price": "$55/mo",
  "detail": "Pay for your phone monthly with an eligible Bell plan.",
  "terms": "24-month term on eligible plans. Plus applicable taxes. Ends Nov 30."
}
```

`category` is `mobile`, `home` or `repairs`. It picks the card's picture and drives the filters on the Deals page. The first three offers also appear on the home page. Offers marked `"status": "example"` show an "Example offer" badge.

To swap a logo, replace the file with the same name and run the build. For example, the business card uses the older "lucky mobile" wordmark: copy `Lucky-Mobile-logo-classic.svg` from the pack over `lucky-mobile.svg` to match it. A `.svg` file is used first, then `.png` (with a `.webp` twin if present). Delete a carrier's file and design 1 falls back to that brand's name and icon. `python3 build.py --logo-placeholders --out preview` shows labelled empty frames for any logo that's missing. Design 2's test holds the approved logo files by their fingerprints (`logos.files` in `rsi/contracts/design-2.json`), so after swapping a logo, update those fingerprints too.

**The home page photo.** Design 2's hero is an AI-generated image, supplied for this preview (a 1942 by 809 pixel PNG, SHA-256 starting `cd2c4f77`; the original file is kept out of the repository, in the folder `.gitignore` lists, and only the cropped versions are published). Which tool made it, and what that tool's terms allow, aren't known, so `sources.json` records its licence as "AI-generated, terms not confirmed" and the design test's licence check fails on purpose until that is settled (see Before launch). The credits page and the footer say it is AI-generated. Its screen shows an iPhone-style home screen: Apple's app icons (the Apple TV icon carries Apple's logo), a weather widget and a map widget with a cartoon avatar. Those belong to other companies, and the test's question about third-party marks can't honestly be answered "none" while this image is on the page. Nothing in it was photographed: it isn't a picture of a real device, so it can suggest a phone the store doesn't sell, which is a second reason to replace it with a photo of a real phone before launch. The picture is shown as supplied, with one change: 70 pixels of plain sky added above the phone (the 48 rows of sky nearest the top edge, mirrored and softened, so nothing is invented and the phone isn't touched). That headroom lets the open/closed tag sit in the sky instead of over the phone's top edge.

**How the hero is cropped.** One picture is cut four ways so that the phone stays the main subject on every screen: 5:4 for phones, held upright or on their side (the phone right of centre, with the open/closed tag in the sky beside it), 2:1 as a banner on tablets (600 to 899 px wide), 1:1 beside the headline on desktops (900 px and wider), and 40:21 for the social preview. The page lets the browser pick the crop that fits (`<picture>` with `media` rules), and the page's CSS gives the frame the same shape as the crop, so the browser never has to crop it a second time. On a phone turned on its side the headline and the photo sit side by side, so the headline isn't pushed below the fold. For this, `rsi/imagery.py` now accepts a supplied photo (provider "Supplied by the client") without a web address or author, still refuses it until its licence is on the contract's allowed list, can extend an edge of a picture (the `extend` setting, which needs a written reason), and labels a picture marked `"ai_generated": true` as AI-generated on the credits page.

**Retouching.** Stock photos show real products with real printing on them. The design test requires that no photo shows a third-party brand name, logo or emblem a visitor could read, so `sources.json` lists what to remove and `imagery.py render` does it to the finished crops; the original files are never changed. Each photo's `retouched` list in `photos.json` records what was removed, and the Photo credits page says so. An earlier run of this check missed several marks that show only at full size (the "Designed by Apple in California" line on the unboxing card, words on a clear phone case and on the repair mat, a "Taptic Engine" label, a faint Apple logo on a white case, and the emblems on the café photo's polo shirt). They were found by reading every photo at full size and by running text recognition over every file the site serves, and they are removed now. A separate agent's read of v4 then found three smaller leftovers (faint lettering on the concert wristband, and on the repair photo's battery label a certification mark, a recycling symbol and small print, plus a faint smudge where the Apple logo had been erased); v6 removes the wristband lettering and the whole battery label, plus two more things found on the way: the picture of a phone printed on the unboxing photo's carton and a dark smear on the train photo's background pole. Earlier runs had answered the test's question about brand marks "yes, none"; that answer was wrong, and the question is answered again for this version. The home page image is the one exception: it shows Apple's app icons on a phone's screen and hasn't been retouched, so it fails that question until it is replaced (see "The home page photo").

**Logos next to photos.** Carrier logos sit unaltered on white plates over the provider, brand and deal photos, but only on photos where nobody can be recognised (hands, a crowd from behind, rooms, parts), so no one appears to endorse a brand or the store. The home page's hero carries the "Plans from" bar of the three logos along its foot. The social preview image (shown when a link is texted) is a plain crop of the hero photograph; no text or logo is added to it.

**Numbers.** Atkinson Hyperlegible Next draws a slashed zero so that 0 and O can't be mistaken for each other, but a slashed zero in a phone number, a price or the hours can look like a printing mistake. So the ten digits, and only the digits (`unicode-range: U+0030-0039`), are drawn by Public Sans, a plain-zero typeface, from a 3 KB subset (`designs/design-2/assets/fonts/public-sans-digits-wght-normal.woff2`, SIL Open Font License, licence file beside it). Letters and punctuation stay in Atkinson. Say if you'd rather have the slashed zero back, or a different typeface.

The design test compares the two designs' wording, and a page that reuses design 1's sentences, headings or navigation labels fails it. A sentence counts as reused when it is at least 60% the same as one of design 1's, so changing a few words isn't enough. Claims the sources didn't support were taken out of the designs ("one bill", "nobody checks your credit", "nothing changes, the plan stays yours"), and what is said about existing Virgin Plus home internet and TV customers is attributed to Bell, whose spokesperson said it.

**Changing a photo.** Everything about a photo lives in `designs/design-2/imagery/sources.json`: where it came from, its licence, who is in it (`none`, `no faces` or `identifiable`), its alt text, its crops (shape, focus point, zoom, widths and, optionally, which file types to write; the link-preview crop is a single JPEG) and any retouch (a box or polygon to fill, with the reason). A retouch fills the area in one of five ways: `smooth` (the default) blends a box from its four edges, `inpaint` uses OpenCV inpainting, `rows` continues each row of a band from its two ends, and `marks` erases only the printed pixels inside a box or polygon, optionally keeping a finger or an edge untouched (`keep`, `protect_sat`), filling from the surface itself or, with `"method": "laplace"`, by smoothly continuing the tone around the mark, and, with `"texture": "copy"`, laying a clean patch's real weave and grain under the fill so it can't be seen. Then (needs Python 3.10+, Pillow, numpy, OpenCV and Playwright with Chromium):

```sh
python3 rsi/imagery.py fetch --design designs/design-2    # download originals and record their fingerprints
python3 rsi/imagery.py render --design designs/design-2   # grade, retouch, crop, encode AVIF/WebP/JPEG, write photos.json
python3 rsi/imagery.py audit --design designs/design-2    # licences, hosts, credits, fingerprints, no faces where none are declared
python3 rsi/imagery.py sheet --design designs/design-2 --out sheet.png   # contact sheet with credits
python3 build.py --design design-2
```

Originals aren't stored in git (`fetch` restores them and checks they're the same files). The rendered photos are, because the site needs them. Only `images.pexels.com` and `images.unsplash.com` are accepted as sources, and paid Unsplash+ images are refused.

## Design 3: photos, logo, wording and layout

**The idea.** "Lamplight" treats the site as a showroom after dark. Pages are near-black with a garnet cast (`#150a0e`), lit in champagne gold (`#dcbd88`), with ivory text and pearl-coloured cards. Headlines are set in Noto Serif Display and text in Jost, both under the SIL Open Font License and slimmed for the web by `designs/design-3/imagery/fonts.py`. Corners are nearly square (2 px). The one ornament, an engine-turned (guilloché) pattern like a watch dial or a banknote, is drawn by `ornaments.py` from a formula, so it has no licence to carry.

They share one colour grade (`grade` in `designs/design-3/imagery/sources.json`), and the house at dusk is toned further toward garnet and champagne (its own `tone` entry, declared on the credits page), so that a dark room, a marble slab and a house at night read as one set. None of them shows a face: one shows a hand (`no faces`), six show no person at all (`none`). The footer and the credits page say the photos are stock photos that don't show the store, its staff or its customers. As in design 2, no photo has a screen, logo or text added; the carrier logos sit beside the photographs, never on them. The Plans page, which is about the three providers, carries no photograph at all. (Earlier builds used eleven photographs; version 4 keeps these seven. The site loads nothing from outside companies, which keeps it fast and private. If you add Google Analytics or a Meta pixel, load them only after the visitor agrees to cookies, and add their domains to the Content-Security-Policy in `src/_headers`, or the browser will block them.

## Design test (WEB-DESIGN-RSI-2.0)

`rsi/` holds a website version of the EMBLEM RSI test suite's PDF design series (PDF-BRIEF-DESIGN-RSI-1.0). The loop: measure a build, review it with evidence, evaluate, then compare it with the version it replaces. A new version is promoted only if its measured layer passes, its review evidence is complete, every score gate passes, its overall score beats the old version by at least 1 point and no dimension drops by more than 3 points.

**Where the three designs stand (October 28, 2026).** Design 1 v9 and design 3 v6 pass every measured check (151 and 315 of them). Design 2 v13 passes 355 of its 357: the two it fails are the licence checks for its AI-generated home page image, which fail on purpose until that image is cleared or replaced (see "The home page photo"). The three builds in this repository and in the demo score 98 to 100 in Lighthouse on every page. None has passed the review gates. The latest independent reads were of the builds before the last round of fixes: design 1 v8 scored 77.8, design 2 v8 77.5 and design 3 v5 74.7, against a gate of 85, and each read failed one or more accuracy questions. The fixes for what those reads found are in v9, v9 and v6 (design 2's v10 changes only its home page image and the footer and credits wording about it, v11 adds Phase 1 of the owner's recommendations, v12 adds the phone makers' logos and v13 a note about cases and screen protectors), but nobody has re-read those builds. Every read so far was done by a separate Claude agent, not a person, using Chromium and Lighthouse only: no Safari or WebKit, no real iPhone, no screen reader. So release stays blocked until a human independent reviewer reads the build that ships and the owner approves it (`"approved_for_production": true` in the contract).

Version 2.0 keeps every 1.0 run valid (design 1's results re-evaluate to the same numbers) and adds what several designs with photography need; version 2.1 of the tool adds the wording and composite checks below. A contract switches each part on:
- **Imagery.** Every photo on a page must be listed in the design's `photos.json` under an allowed licence, served as AVIF or WebP with a JPEG fallback for older iPhones, sharp but not oversized for its shown size, within a byte budget, lazy below the first screen, with the biggest photo on the first screen loaded first. The imagery audit (licences, sources, fingerprints, and a face detector confirming that photos declared faceless show no faces) must pass, and the build must carry the design's current `photos.json`. A contract can also cap composited photos per page (designs 2 and 3 allow none) and require that the hero is a real photograph.
- **Logos.** Each page shows the logos its role needs; logo files are byte-for-byte the approved ones; each logo is drawn big enough, undistorted and on a clean plate (white in design 2, pearl in design 3); no logo sits on a photo of someone who can be recognised.
- **Distinctness.** The design's first screens (phone and desktop), palette and typefaces are compared with the other designs' runs: structural similarity of the first screens at most 0.45, palette distance at least 12, a different headline typeface. Calibration: two builds of design 1 that differ below the fold score 1.0 and 0; design 1's first version against its redesign scores 0.16 and 8.6 (same design family, so not distinct enough).
- **Wording.** Two designs for one store must say the same facts but not the same sentences. `measure` (against each sibling design named in the contract) and `distinct` take the shared facts out (store details, hours, offers, carriers, listed program and place names) and compare what is left: how many four-word phrases the two designs share, how many whole sentences are reused, how many headings and navigation labels match, and how alike each page's title, description, heading and lead are. Designs 2 and 3's contracts allow at most 0.15 phrase overlap, 0.05 reused sentences (a sentence is reused when it is 0.6 alike to one of the sibling's; the tool's default is 0.85), 0.15 shared headings and 3 shared navigation labels, and no page field more than 0.7 alike. `python3 rsi/web_design_rsi.py copy --a <run or build> --b <run or build> --contract ...` shows the comparison on its own and lists every flagged page.
- **Accuracy questions from the contract.** The reviews of designs 2 and 3 also answer: are the photos licensed for commercial use; does any photo imply it shows the store, staff or customers; are carrier logos unaltered on clean plates; are there third-party brand marks in the photos; was the wording written for this design rather than reused from another.
- **Evidence.** Each page is scrolled so lazy photos load before full-page screenshots; the first phone screen of each page, the imagery contact sheet and the distinctness screenshots are kept too.

Some checks are done by hand, because the harness doesn't measure them: pressing Tab through every page at 320, 360 and 390 pixels wide to confirm that the focused link is never hidden behind the fixed bottom bar or the sticky header (it wasn't in any design's v9 or v6, after the independent reads found it was in designs 2 and 3), true no-script rendering, clock-simulated open and closed lines, a crawl of every link, anchor and image, and html-validate.

### Independent reads

An independent read is a separate Claude agent that gets the built site and its measurements but not the producer's scores, drives Chromium itself (phone sizes from 320 to 430 px, landscape, desktop to 1920 px, no-script, keyboard, clock-simulated open and closed lines, Lighthouse, html-validate, text recognition on the photos), compares the wording with the other designs sentence by sentence, and writes a review in the test's format. It saw only what Chromium shows: no Safari, no real iPhone, no screen reader, and none of the pages on `pexels.com` or the carriers' own sites that the workspace's network blocked. The scores below are the review's, run through the test's own evaluator. They are not a human's.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | v8 | 77.8 | Content matches sources (two lines stronger than, or misattributed to, their sources; the SmartPay lines left out a Bell condition; the hours note misstated Google); unverified claims flagged for the owner |
| 2 | v4 | 75.7 | Offers and reviews not fabricated; unverified claims flagged; third-party marks in photos; wording written for this design |

| 3 | v5 | 74.7 | Unverified claims flagged (promises the owner's list didn't name); third-party marks in photos (a gold maker's mark on the circuit board) |

What the reads kept finding, most costly first, and where it stands:
- **The deals are examples.** All five offers are placeholders (`$XX`) labelled "Example offer"; the release build refuses to run until they are real. This holds the Deals page's information score down in every design.
- **The hours and the postal code are shown as fact on every page, but other listings disagree.** The brief, the site and Bell's store locator say 10 a.m. to 8 p.m. every day; the Google listing shows Sunday as 10:30 a.m. to 7 p.m., and the postal code is L6X 0G3 on the business card and on Google but L6X 5K7 on Bell's locator. Both are on the owner's list, and the open and closed lines follow whatever hours the file holds.
- **Photos are stock.** None shows the store. The footer and credits say so, but real photographs of the counter and the repair bench would score better on imagery and trust. Design 2 uses four stock photos of recognisable people without a model release on Pexels' pages. Design 2's home page image is AI-generated, was supplied without its tool or terms, and shows Apple's app icons on its screen, so it has to be cleared or replaced before launch.
- **The carrier logos came from the brands' public websites, not Bell's dealer kit**, and the T Wireless wordmark in design 3 is redrawn in gold. Both need the owner's or Bell's approval.
- **Keyboard focus hidden behind the fixed bottom bar** (WCAG 2.2, 2.4.11), which Lighthouse doesn't test, in designs 2 and 3 (and design 1 on some phones): fixed by `scroll-padding-bottom` in all three.
- **Wording that went beyond the sources, or promised something the owner hadn't confirmed**: reworded, or added to the "Before launch" list.
- **Things the reads could not check**: Safari and WebKit, a real phone, a screen reader, form delivery (the demo host cannot receive forms), where "Open in Google Maps" lands, whether the store may sell all three carriers, and the carriers' own pages for Virgin Plus and Lucky Mobile.

All three designs pass html-validate's recommended rules except two style rules left off on purpose: one asks for a non-breaking hyphen and spaces inside every phone number (that would stop a search for the number with ordinary hyphens from finding it on the page), the other limits titles to 70 characters (one of design 1's run 76, six of design 2's run 72 to 81, none of design 3's; search engines may shorten the longer ones). Every internal link, anchor and icon reference resolves, and no page scrolls sideways at the eight window sizes checked (phones 320, 360, 390 and 430 pixels wide, a landscape phone, then 1024, 1440 and 1920). Each run folder keeps the exact build it measured (`site/`) and its hashed screenshots (`evidence/`), so `evaluate` and `compare` can re-check them anywhere.

### Running the test

Needs Python 3.10+, Playwright for Python with Chromium, Lighthouse, and for 2.0 contracts Pillow, numpy and OpenCV. To clear a release block, an independent reviewer scores the build that ships from its screenshots:

```sh
python3 rsi/web_design_rsi.py template --measured rsi/runs/design-3/v6-candidate/measured.json --contract rsi/contracts/design-3.json --reviewer-id REVIEWER-NAME --reviewer-role independent_reviewer --out rsi/runs/design-3/v6-candidate/review-independent.json
# fill in each 0-5 score and rationale, set "status": "reviewed", answer the accuracy questions
python3 rsi/web_design_rsi.py evaluate --contract rsi/contracts/design-3.json --measured rsi/runs/design-3/v6-candidate/measured.json --review rsi/runs/design-3/v6-candidate/review-independent.json --out rsi/runs/design-3/v6-candidate/result-independent.json
```

For design 1 use `rsi/contracts/design-1.json` and `rsi/runs/design-1/v9-candidate/`; for design 2 use `rsi/contracts/design-2.json` and its newest run, `rsi/runs/design-2/v13-candidate/` (which can't pass until the home page photo is cleared or replaced and the build is measured again). Then set `"approved_for_production": true` in the contract once the owner signs off. To test a later change to design 3:

```sh
python3 build.py --design design-3
python3 rsi/web_design_rsi.py measure --site dist-design-3 --contract rsi/contracts/design-3.json --out rsi/runs/design-3/v7-candidate
python3 rsi/web_design_rsi.py template --measured rsi/runs/design-3/v7-candidate/measured.json --contract rsi/contracts/design-3.json --reviewer-id REVIEWER-NAME --out rsi/runs/design-3/v7-candidate/review.json
# fill in the review, then compare with the build it replaces (v6)
python3 rsi/web_design_rsi.py compare --contract rsi/contracts/design-3.json --incumbent-measured rsi/runs/design-3/v6-candidate/measured.json --incumbent-review rsi/runs/design-3/v6-candidate/review.json --measured rsi/runs/design-3/v7-candidate/measured.json --review rsi/runs/design-3/v7-candidate/review.json --out rsi/runs/design-3/v7-candidate/promotion.json
```

`measure` copies the build into the run, so `dist-design-3/` can be rebuilt afterwards. The contract names the sibling runs (`distinctness.siblings`) that the wording and look are compared with; point them at the newest runs of the other designs. To see how different any two designs look: `python3 rsi/web_design_rsi.py distinct --run-a rsi/runs/design-3/v6-candidate --run-b rsi/runs/design-1/v9-candidate --contract rsi/contracts/design-3.json`. The wording numbers read "how much of A's wording is in B", so put the design you're checking first (design 3 against design 1 gives 3.0% of phrases, design 1 against design 3 gives 3.7%).

Scores are reviewer observations backed by Lighthouse and browser measurements. They are not a measure of how visitors behave.
