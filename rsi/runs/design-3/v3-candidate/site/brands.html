Paying for a handset with Bell

Keeping the handset you own is perfectly possible, since a plan can be set up on it. When you do want a new one, Bell offers two ways to avoid paying for it in one sum, and we arrange both in the store.

  • Paying in instalments. Bell SmartPay divides a handset’s price into 24 monthly payments. There is no down payment, and no interest is charged. Approval rests on Bell’s credit check, and only an eligible 2-year plan qualifies.
  • Handing the handset back. The Device Return Option is open to eligible Bell handsets. A portion of the price is held over for the length of a 2-year term. At the end, a handset returned in good working condition ends the matter, and the held portion is waived. Keeping the handset means paying that amount.
  • Trade-in value. Your present handset may be worth something against the next one; ask us how a trade-in works.

What the other two bring

Both run on Bell’s mobile network. Where they part company is the way you pay and the extras each plan carries.

Virgin Plus

Virgin Plus belongs to Bell and sells monthly plans. Member Benefits come with most of them, as a set of perks you do not pay extra for.

  • Where to find the perks. They live in the My Benefits app, across shopping, food, entertainment and travel. There are no points to earn, and the selection changes over time.
  • Joining from elsewhere. Bring your number across when you join.

Lucky Mobile

Lucky Mobile is the prepaid brand from Bell. Payment comes first and service follows, so no invoice is left to settle when the month ends.

  • Nothing to sign. A credit check is not part of joining, and neither is a long contract.
  • No surprise data bills. Overage charges do not apply to data. Ask how a plan behaves after its full-speed allowance is spent.

Two changes to know about

January 15, 2027: Virgin Plus prepaid ends. A customer who moves to a monthly plan beforehand keeps their number. We can make that move for you in the store, or lay Lucky Mobile prepaid alongside it for comparison.

Virgin Plus at home. Since January 14, 2026, a household in Ontario that is not already a customer cannot start Virgin Plus home internet or TV. According to Virgin Plus, anyone who is already a customer may continue as they are, on the same plan, price and equipment. A new home starts with its address: we look it up and tell you which Bell services can reach it.

Beyond the phone

Bell home services

Take internet, TV or a home phone line singly, or combine them. Whether each one is offered depends on your address.

  • Bell Internet. An internet connection for the household; fibre is offered at addresses covered by Bell’s fibre network.
  • Fibe TV. Channels play live or from the on-demand library through the Fibe TV app. The service requires Bell Internet.
  • Satellite TV. Bell television that arrives by satellite and is received on a dish.
  • Home Phone. A fixed line into the house. It can be the only service, or sit beside internet and TV.
How a home setup works

Bell for business

A small business can equip its staff from one place: Bell handsets and mobile plans are arranged here in the store.

  • Handsets for the team. Business smartphones qualify for Bell SmartPay too, on the same terms. Ask us how a team order works.
  • Start the conversation. Tell us the size of your team and how the handsets will be used, and bring your company details. We will plan the rest with you.

Plans, prices and terms belong to the providers and are subject to change, so ask us for current details before you decide.
